Before We Build

Accumulated feedback already exists in the app. Your document changes how it is counted — from a share of samples against a fixed percentage, to measured minutes against RAMP duration bands, plus an end-of-shift forecast. Almost all of it is clear enough to build from.

These are the points where we would have to guess, and guessing wrong means rework. Each one says what the app does today and what we will assume if we do not hear back.

The one that costs the most

The voice today is nine recorded clips. The new design needs sentences.

Right now the app plays a fixed MP3 per limb per colour — left arm, right arm, trunk × green, yellow, red, in English and Swedish. The new messages contain live numbers ("still high — 35 minutes"), technique advice, praise and escalation. That cannot be pre-recorded in any sensible number of files. Question 4 is where most of the effort in this feature sits.

The bands

1What are the duration bands for trunk and head?Blocker

Today: trunk and head both give accumulated feedback, on their own hardcoded percentages.

The document gives one verified table — RAMP II item 1.5, arm at or above shoulder. There is nothing equivalent for forward bending of the trunk, backward bending, or head down. We cannot invent those numbers; they need to come from you or from a RAMP source we can quote.

If they are not available, the alternative is to convert the arm only and leave trunk and head on the current logic for now — but then two body parts on the same screen are scored two different ways, which we would rather avoid.

2Which arm angle counts as "hand above shoulder"?Blocker

Today: the arm has two bands — above 30° and above 60° — each with its own percentage limit.

RAMP has one condition: hand at or above shoulder height. So we need one angle. Is it 60°, or higher? And what happens to the 30–60° band — does it get its own duration table, or does it stop driving the colour and become display only?

3Confirm the new bands are far stricter than today.

Today: the arm turns red at 50 % of the time above 30°, or 10 % above 60°.

RAMP turns yellow at 30 minutes above shoulder in a day (about 6 %) and red at one hour (about 12.5 %). The same worker doing the same job will go red much earlier and much more often than they do now. That is the point of the change, but it will look like a regression to anyone using the app today, so we want it confirmed before we ship it.

The voice

4Recorded clips, or text-to-speech?Blocker

Today: nine MP3 files per language, one per limb per colour. No head clips at all.

Three ways to do the new messages:

a. Switch to text-to-speech on the phone. Any sentence, any number, easy to change later, works for every language — but a synthetic voice, and it needs testing on the noise of a real floor.

b. Record a larger fixed set. Natural voice, but it is roughly 30–40 clips per body part per language, minutes cannot be spoken, and every wording change means going back to the studio.

c. Keep the three clips per limb and only change the counting. Cheapest by far, and the calculation still improves — but none of the coaching design in the document happens.

If we hear nothing

Text-to-speech, English and Swedish, with the sentence text kept in the app so wording can be changed without a new build.

5Confirm the voice slows down a lot.

Today: the voice repeats every 30 seconds by default, after a 10-minute warm-up.

The document says one message every 10 minutes, at most six an hour, about one body part only — the worst one — and never two warnings at once. That is a very different experience. We think it is right, but it is a big change for anyone used to the current app.

If we hear nothing

We build 10 minutes as the default and leave the interval adjustable in settings, as it is today.

Time and scope

6What time is excluded from the count?

Today: every sample counts; there is a pause on the session timer but it does not affect the feedback.

The document says the denominator is valid working time — minus breaks, minus any period with no usable sensor data. We plan to treat a paused session as a break, drop sensor-dropout time out of the count entirely, and keep the voice silent while paused.

Worth confirming, because it means a session with a lot of dropout will now show a higher percentage than it does today for the same work.

7Does the forecast split by task, or only by session?

Today: the app has work tasks and work cycles, but feedback is counted across the whole session.

The forecast is much better if it knows the current task is overhead work for one more hour and then bench work for six. The document treats that as first-class. The question is whether we reuse the existing work task and cycle structure for it in this round, or forecast from the whole session only and add tasks later.

If we hear nothing

Session-scope forecast in this round, built so task scope can be added on top without redoing it.

8How long is the shift, for the forecast?

Today: there is a session duration limit setting, default 8 hours.

The forecast needs planned working hours, not elapsed session time. We plan to reuse that 8-hour setting and assume 7 hours of actual work in it. If shifts vary by customer this should become a proper setting instead.

The report

9Does the session report change too?

Today: the report shows time and percentage per angle band, calculated separately from the live feedback.

Two things to decide. First, whether the report should now also show the projected shift exposure and the RAMP colour, or stay as it is. Second, whether the report has to count breaks and dropouts exactly the way the live feedback will — because if it does not, a worker who was shown red all afternoon can open a report that says something different.

If we hear nothing

We align the report's time handling with the live calculation and leave the report layout alone for now.
