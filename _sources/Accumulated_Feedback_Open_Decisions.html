Six Decisions
Before We Build

Accumulated feedback already exists in the app. Your document changes how it is counted — measured minutes against RAMP duration bands instead of a share of samples against a fixed percentage, plus an end-of-shift forecast. Almost everything needed is either in the document or already in the app.

These six are what is left. Every other question we had turned out to be answered — either by the document or by something the app already does. That list is at the end so you can see what we are not asking.

The bands

1What are the duration bands for trunk and head?Blocker

In the app today: trunk and head both give accumulated feedback, on their own fixed percentages.

The document gives one verified table — RAMP II item 1.5, arm at or above shoulder — and says the table must be stored per item, never globally. There is nothing equivalent for forward bending of the trunk, backward bending, or head down, and your own open-questions list says the per-item tables are not yet decided. We cannot invent them.

If they are not available yet, the alternative is to convert the arm only and leave trunk and head on the current logic. But then two body parts on the same screen are scored two different ways, which we would rather avoid.

2Which arm elevation angle counts as "hand above shoulder"?Blocker

In the app today: the arm has two bands, above 30° and above 60°, each with its own limit.

The document is explicit that RAMP defines this by hand height and that we measure arm angle, and lists the elbow-angle assumption as something each variable must document — but it never gives the angle. RAMP has one condition, so we need one number.

And what happens to the 30–60° band? Does it get its own duration table, or does it stop driving the colour and become display only?

The voice

3Synthetic voice, and who writes the sentences?Blocker

In the app today: nine recorded MP3s per language — left arm, right arm, trunk × green, yellow, red. No head clips at all.

The new messages contain live numbers ("still high — 35 minutes") and vary by event. That cannot be pre-recorded, so it has to be text-to-speech — a synthetic voice replacing the recorded one the app uses now. We read the document as already assuming that, but it is the thing a worker will notice most, so we want it said out loud before we build it.

Separately, your open-questions list leaves the final wording and localisation undecided. These sentences are the feature — a clumsy one spoken every ten minutes gets it switched off. Who writes and approves the English and Swedish?

Time and the forecast

4How is a break logged?

In the app today: there is no break. A pause exists in the code but nothing in the app calls it.

The document defines valid time as elapsed minus logged breaks minus sensor dropout, and says the voice must stay quiet during a break. Dropout we can handle ourselves. Breaks have nothing to log them.

So: do we add a pause button to the recording screen, or do we say a break means stopping the session, and leave it out of this round?

If we hear nothing

We add a pause button, count paused time out of the denominator, and keep the voice silent while paused.

5What fills the rest of the shift in the forecast?Blocker

In the app today: a session is already split into work tasks with expected durations and a timeline — but that plan only covers the recording, not the whole day.

The forecast projects to an 8-hour workday. The app knows the tasks it is recording; it knows nothing about the other six or seven hours. Your open-questions list flags exactly this — the source of remaining planned time — and offers falling back to whole-session scope.

Two options. a. Assume the rest of the day looks like what we measured, and extrapolate over 7 active hours. Needs nothing new, but a 20-minute recording of the worst task in the day becomes a red 8-hour projection. b. Ask the ergonomist what fills the rest of the shift when setting up the session. More honest, more setup.

If we hear nothing

Option a, using the existing task structure for the recorded part, with the projection clearly marked as an estimate from a partial recording.

The report

6Does the projection go into the session report, and per arm or worst arm?

In the app today: the report already shows time and percentage per angle band, calculated separately from the live feedback.

The document settles that the report and the live view must count breaks and dropouts identically — we will align them. What it leaves open is whether the report should also carry the projected shift exposure and the RAMP colour, and whether it shows both arms or only the worse one. RAMP itself scores only the worse arm; an ergonomist usually wants to see both.

What we are not asking, and why

Question we droppedAlready answered by
How much stricter the new bands are The document. It states RAMP is yellow at 30 min and red at 1 h, calls the old 10 % / 30 % bands far more permissive, and sets RAMP-informed as the default.
One threshold set or two The document. RAMP-informed by default, with the looser set kept and clearly labelled as training thresholds.
Whether we may call the output RAMP The document. Until it is validated against observation it is described as a RAMP-aligned sensor proxy.
How often the voice speaks The document. Ten minutes, at most six an hour, one body part — the worst. The app already has the interval and warm-up as settings.
What time is excluded from the count The document. Elapsed minus breaks minus any period with no valid sensor sample, counted in seconds, never in samples. Only the break part needed asking — question 4.
Where shift length comes from The document sets 8 h planned, 7 h active. The app already has an 8-hour session limit setting to hang it on.
Whether the forecast splits by task The app. Work tasks with expected durations and a timeline already exist, so we use them. Only the unrecorded rest of the shift needed asking — question 5.
Customer-written coaching messages The document marks the whole message-library section as future scope, not part of this release.
